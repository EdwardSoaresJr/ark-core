# Workspace Evolution Notebook

**Status:** Era 3 operational tool — observe, don't invent  
**Constitution:** [ark-constitution-v1.md](../ecosystem/ark-constitution-v1.md)

The next milestone is not a commit. It is a **sentence**.

This notebook is **ARK's memory about itself** — not code, not analytics. Institutional memory. Six months from now, *"Why does Save & Next work this way?"* is answered by *"Landon said, 'I kept losing my place after every photo.'"* — richer than git history.

Protect the sentence **"I don't know yet."** The constitution should make that easier to say. It is not indecision. It is respecting the difference between an observation, a pattern, and a product decision.

---

## Four kinds of evidence

Not four mandatory sections — **four ways to read what you record**:

| Kind | Tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Repeated sentences** | Where people struggled |
| **Silent successes** | What disappeared |
| **Interesting, not earned** | Protects curiosity |
| **Unexpected behaviors** | What people invented |

### Unexpected behaviors

Moments someone uses ARK in a way you never designed for — but that may reveal a **better workflow**.

Examples:

- Landon always opens Conversations before starting inspection
- Molly never uses the command bar because she long-presses the header
- A technician takes all photos first, then measurements

**Don't fix these immediately.** Ask why they happened. Sometimes users discover a better workflow than the designer.

| Date | Who | Unexpected behavior | Why might this work? | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | | Observe / Ask / Tighten later |

## After Landon's week — three questions

Do not ask *"How do you like it?"*

Ask:

1. **When did ARK make you hesitate?**
2. **When did ARK disappear?**
3. **What did you expect to happen that didn't?**

Question 2 is the one that matters most.

> *"I stopped thinking about the app."*

Software is rarely praised for disappearing. For a technician, that is the highest compliment.

End-of-day hesitation question (still valid):

> **Did ARK ever make you uncertain about what to do next?**

## Monthly — Delete more than you add

Development habit (not doctrine). Once a month ask:

- What **screen** disappeared?
- What **button** disappeared?
- What **explanation** disappeared?
- What **setting** disappeared?
- What **training** disappeared?

Those are product victories, not losses.

If ARK becomes more capable while requiring less explanation, the constitution is working.

## Six-month metric

Not Flutter. Not Laravel. Not Workspace Inspector.

> **"Landon didn't need to ask."**

That's the metric.

A year from now, the competitive advantage may not be any single feature. It will be that everything feels designed by the same mind. Customers won't name the doctrine. They'll say: **"Everything just works the way I expect."** That's what coherence feels like from the outside.

The notebook is not a pause in development. It is the first time the **shop itself becomes a contributor** to the product.
