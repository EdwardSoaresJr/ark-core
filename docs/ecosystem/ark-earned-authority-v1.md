# Earned Authority v1

**Status:** Foundational — governs everything that leaves the shop on behalf of ARK  
**Not:** A marketing doctrine, SEO playbook, or content calendar  
**Applies to:** Public website · ARK Companion · advisor AI · voice AI · ARKademy · analytics · customer summaries · reports · social · email · any surface where ARK speaks for the shop

---

## Principle

**Authority is not created by publishing.**

**Authority is earned through observation.**

> **Naming:** **Earned Authority** (this document) = when knowledge may **leave** the shop. **Market Authority** ([ark-market-authority-v1.md](./ark-market-authority-v1.md)) = trust the **market** grants the business (business health). Do not conflate the two uses of "authority."

Every statement made by ARK or the shop should be traceable to operational truth.

> **Every public sentence should have a repair order standing behind it.**

Not literally one RO per sentence — but conceptually. If someone challenged a claim, the shop should be able to say *"Here's why we know that"* — not *"Every SEO guide says to write that."*

---

## Knowledge progression

Earned Authority is the **gatekeeper** between internal truth and the outside world. It completes a progression the platform has been building toward:

```
Pressure
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Vocabulary
    ↓
Authority
    ↓
Projection
    ↓
Earned Authority  ← when knowledge is allowed to leave
```

The first five describe how knowledge is **created** inside the shop.  
The last describes when knowledge is **allowed to leave**.

---

## Sources of authority

A statement is authoritative when it is derived from one or more of:

| Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Verified repair outcomes** | Closed work, diagnostic results, parts replaced, measured resolution |
| **Repeated customer questions** | The same sentence asked on the floor, in intake, or in comms — not once |
| **Documented diagnostic procedures** | How this shop actually tests — not generic industry copy |
| **Measured operational data** | Counts, rates, ages, dollars — with a defined authority path |
| **Explicit shop policy** | Owner-entered policy the shop stands behind (warranty terms, hours, billing class rules) |

**No other source creates authority.**

Inference, competitor mimicry, SEO templates, and AI fluency are not sources.

---

## Publication rule

**Public knowledge is a projection of operational truth.**

Projection must never invent, exaggerate, or infer beyond its authority.

| Rule | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Project once** | Package answers per render or per audience — do not re-derive inline |
| **Never invent** | If the shop has not observed it, do not publish it |
| **Never exaggerate** | Rounded marketing numbers without verified counts are fabrication |
| **Publish uncertainty** | When the shop does not know yet, say so — that is trustworthy |

Companion: ark-projection-rule.mdc · ark-explainability-doctrine.mdc

**Explainability Rule:** If evidence cannot be shown, the claim is not operational truth — hold it in engineering detail or the notebook, not on the floor or the public site.

## The product loop

The website is not the product. The website is **where the public experiences earned knowledge**.

The product is the full loop:

```
Shop
  ↓
ARK
  ↓
Observations
  ↓
Knowledge
  ↓
Website (and every outbound surface)
  ↓
New customers
  ↓
Shop
```

Growth is the publication arm of operational truth — not a marketing layer bolted onto operations.

## Feedback loop

```
Operational Event
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Vocabulary
    ↓
Authority
    ↓
Projection
    ↓
Customer Interaction
    ↓
New Operational Events
```

**Authority compounds.** A shop that documents what it learns publishes what competitors cannot copy without running the same floor for years.

Example path (public website today):

```
Customer searches → Problem authority page → Talk to a service advisor
    → ARK intake → Repair order → Repair outcome → Observation
    → Future authority page ("What we see at our shop")
```

The **"What we see at our shop"** block stays hidden until ARK earns the data. Empty is better than fabricated.

---

## Rejection test

Reject any content that:

- exists only because competitors have it
- exists only for SEO
- cannot be traced to shop operations
- replaces observation with assumption
- fabricates confidence (invented repair counts, generic "we've seen thousands" claims)

**Litmus:**

> **If we deleted this sentence tomorrow, would any operational record miss it?**

If no — it was probably publication, not authority.

---

## Success metric

The goal is not to publish more.

The goal is to **reduce the distance between what the shop learns and what the public can trust.**

| Wrong metric | Right metric |
| --- | --- |
| Pages shipped | Questions earned from the floor |
| Traffic | Work attributed through the loop |
| Content calendar velocity | Verified outcomes enriching authorities |
| Homepage redesigns | Stability until observation demands change |

## Public marketing v1 — closed

**Closed:** 2026-07-06 · Commit `8a37017a` and doctrine alignment

Public marketing v1 is **closed** — not because the site is perfect, but because the foundation is coherent:

- One customer application, problem authorities, advisor intake, operational footer
- CTA discipline, trust signals, publication paths (warranty, privacy, terms)
- Problem authority template with shop-experience hook (hidden until earned)

**From here forward, the public site should not evolve because someone has a new layout idea.**

It should evolve because **the shop earned something new to say.**

Homepage layout is **frozen** until analytics or repeated observation proves a specific failure. Stability has value once architecture is sound.

## Engineering allocation (guidance)

When planning engineering time after v1:

| Share | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **~40%** | New authority pages — earned from actual customer questions, not AI-generated filler |
| **~30%** | ARK observation capture — completed repairs enrich the knowledge base |
| **~20%** | Diagnostic tools — utilities people use before calling, not gimmicks |
| **~10%** | UI polish — only when observation says it matters |

## Closing

> **You're not trying to build the prettiest auto repair website in Demo City. You're trying to build the most trustworthy automotive knowledge source in Demo City.**

That is harder to copy — because it depends on years of real repairs, real diagnostics, and real observations, not better web design.

Read this before publishing, before prompting AI for customer copy, and before approving any surface that speaks on behalf of the shop.
